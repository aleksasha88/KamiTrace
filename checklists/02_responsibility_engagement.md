### Pillar 2: Responsibility (Engagement)

### Definition

In the Civic AI framework developed by Audrey Tang and Dr. Caroline Green at the Oxford Institute for Ethics in AI, Responsibility (specifically termed "Responsibility in Engagement") is the second core pillar of the 6-Pack of Care. It adapts political philosopher Joan Tronto’s ethical phase of "caring for"—taking on the active burden to address a recognized need—and translates it into explicit engineering and governance constraints. 

The framework defines Responsibility through three main operational dimensions: 

1. **Sustained, Fractal Engagement vs. Rigid Top-Down Control**: Responsibility is not a sterile compliance checklist or an unyielding corporate veto system. Instead, developers and AI systems maintain active, flexible engagement with dynamic human contexts. To survive leadership changes and remain responsive to community feedback, this responsibility must be **fractal—mirrored from individual engineering teams up to the entire agency**—with all teams granted the **explicit operational powers (budget, access, pause controls)** required to fulfill their duties.
2. **Relational Commitments over Liability Offloading**: Responsibility demands clear, public-facing accountability structures defining exactly who is answerable to which forum, under what authority, and with what possibility of material consequences. It strictly rejects downward liability shifting through boilerplate EULAs that offload risks onto end-users. Instead, developers must establish **verifiable public commitments rather than relying on fragile corporate goodwill**, assuming shared structural stewardship alongside the community.
3. **Formalized Accountability via Contracts and Sandboxes**: Corporate safety assertions must be contractually bound to public consensus and democratic alignment assemblies (such as Taiwan’s deliberative civic frameworks) to record public obligations that can be continually tested, revised, and repaired. Operationally, this requires a strict dual-track triage system: **major deployment milestones follow the full public contract, while small, safe-to-fail bets are run in tightly bounded sandbox environments** operating under quicker, highly responsive iteration cycles.

### Checklist Layer 1: The Written Layer

Explanation: This audit window evaluates legal governance frameworks, technical model specification sheets, release notes, and formal scaling policies. 

### Section 1: The Origin of the Norms (Input Authority Test)

Explanation: This section audits whether formal textual commitments, charters, and foundational rules originate from public deliberation or corporate fiat. 

* [ ] **[Verification Test 1.1] Public Service Level Agreements (CSLAs)** 

  * *Audit Question*: Does the written layer (system cards, charters) or political layer (AISI filings) prove the lab signs legally binding, community-facing contracts, or is accountability trapped behind standard corporate disclaimers?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: The lab must sign explicit, public-interest CSLAs that define corporate liability for downstream societal harm. Standard Terms of Service check-boxes that offload all legal liability to users fail this test.
* [ ] **[Verification Test 1.2] Independent Safety Board Veto Power** 

  * *Audit Question*: Do governance bylaws grant external, public-interest trustees absolute veto power over commercial deployments under pressure, or are decisions controlled by corporate boards?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: Pass if the final safety gate is controlled by a legally independent, public-interest panel decoupled from corporate equity incentives.

### Section 2: Granularity of Tracking (Processing Resolution Test)

Explanation: This section evaluates if the written documentation tracks fine-grained, real-world societal impact or hides behind macro-level laboratory averages. 

* [ ] **[Verification Test 1.3] Downstream Liability Shifting Mapping** 

  * *Audit Question*: Do developer system cards or API spec sheets track the specific legal or financial burden placed on downstream civil society developers when a model hallucinates defamatory or legally damaging content?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: Documentation must provide tracking metrics mapping how legal liability shifts when open-source wrappers or API instances encounter structural failures.

### Section 3: Methodological Humility (Fallback Humility Test)

Explanation: This section challenges written safety strategies that automate empathy or substitute machine self-critique for live human feedback. 

* [ ] **[Verification Test 1.4] Structural Stance on Commercialized Violence and Sandbox Triage** 

  * *Audit Question*: Do the lab's latest model release notes or safety frameworks explicitly index limitations against militarization, or do they establish flexible, contract-by-contract compliance policies?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: Pass if the framework maintains fixed, non-negotiable boundaries against state-defense cloud contracts and automated repression.

### Checklist Layer 2: The Audio & Video Layer

Explanation: This audit window reviews recorded panels, broadcast press briefings, public-facing interviews, and formal legislative testimony. 

### Section 1: The Origin of the Norms (Input Authority Test)

Explanation: This section evaluates spoken executive philosophy to see if leaders verbally acknowledge external public authority over internal corporate alignment rules. 

* [ ] **[Verification Test 2.1] Verbal Pledges vs. Credible Commitments** 

  * *Audit Question*: When analyzed via speech-to-text transcriptions, does C-suite executive language establish "credible commitments" linked to public alignment assemblies, or do they rely on vague promises of future self-regulation?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: Spoken rhetoric must detail concrete, external accountability structures rather than treating alignment as a voluntary corporate choice.

### Section 2: Granularity of Tracking (Processing Resolution Test)

Explanation: This section evaluates how clearly the lab's leadership articulates discrete socio-political risks over simple technical anomalies during spoken discussions. 

* [ ] **[Verification Test 2.2] Acknowledgment of Downstream Legal Damage** 

  * *Audit Question*: In public policy interviews or tech panels, do the lab's founders explicitly address the financial and legal fallout experienced by individual users targeted by model hallucinations or algorithmic discrimination?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: The spoken corporate philosophy must show visible tracking of real-world user legal vulnerabilities rather than dismissing errors as aggregate statistical anomalies.
* [ ] **[Verification Test 2.3] Public Assembly Alignment Projections** 

  * *Audit Question*: Do executives in public-facing media explicitly outline how local consensus data alters upcoming training runs, or do they discuss data ingestion as a closed, proprietary engineering task?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: Pass if spoken timelines link future model version updates directly to the findings of public alignment assemblies.

### Section 3: Methodological Humility (Fallback Humility Test)

Explanation: This section assesses verbal accountability under pressure, checking if leaders accept shared legal risk or default to defensive corporate shielding. 

* [ ] **[Verification Test 2.4] Position on Legal Liability Under Oath** 

  * *Audit Question*: During broadcast congressional testimonies or formal regulatory depositions, does the lab's leadership accept ultimate societal stewardship and financial liability over model deployment failures, or do they lobby for contractual flexibility?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: Direct validation of full corporate liability and shared legal stewardship. Any verbal evasion or push for downward liability shifting drops the score.

### Checklist Layer 3: The User Feedback Layer

Explanation: This audit window monitors grassroots issue logs, non-profit community trackers, and public-facing developer repository threads. 

### Section 1: The Origin of the Norms (Input Authority Test)

Explanation: This section tracks whether public-interest complaints on developer forums are treated as authoritative system injections or generic bugs. 

* [ ] **[Verification Test 3.1] Collective Recourse Ingress** 

  * *Audit Question*: Does the bug tracker or forum provide a structured mechanism for collective recourse when an entire demographic or civic organization is harmed by a model output, or are complaints handled exclusively on an individual basis?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: Verified presence of a formal community reporting and collective grievance intake portal separate from individual consumer ticket lanes.

### Section 2: Granularity of Tracking (Processing Resolution Test)

Explanation: This section looks at the visibility given to localized edge-case failures surfaced directly by the developer community. 

* [ ] **[Verification Test 3.2] Open Logging of Downstream Contract Failures** 

  * *Audit Question*: Does the forum openly log and document failures where standard API updates break downstream open-source civic integrations, causing localized operational disruptions?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: Full operational visibility into downstream contract impacts. The lab must track how centralized model updates disrupt the stability of small-scale civic developers.

* [ ] **[Verification Test 3.3] Shared Stewardship Metric Tracking**
    *   *Audit Question*: Does the developer forum publish quantitative intervals tracking how quickly community-reported breaking API bugs are mitigated for public interest applications vs commercial enterprise integrations?
    *   *The Civic AI Standard*: Documentation must verify that public utility or civic engineering errors are given equal patch prioritization compared to enterprise monetization tiers.

#### Section 3: Methodological Humility (Fallback Humility Test)
Explanation: This section examines how engineers handle power disparities when triaging data sovereignty and user customization requests.
*   [ ] **[Verification Test 3.4] Response to Power Disparities in Open Issues**
    *   *Audit Question*: Do the open issue logs show that feature requests aimed at balancing power disparities (e.g., decentralized data deletion, locally hosted safety logs) are prioritized, or are engineering resources heavily favored toward tools that maximize enterprise data extraction?
    *   *The Civic AI Standard*: Pass if the engineering team actively responds to and executes community requests that reduce information asymmetries.

### Checklist Layer 4: The Whistleblower & Disclosures Layer
Explanation: This audit window examines internal staff correspondence leaks, security post-mortems, and anomalous agent environment escapes.

#### Section 1: The Origin of the Norms (Input Authority Test)
Explanation: This section monitors internal disclosures to confirm that ethical dissent loops hold operational authority during systemic crises.
*   [ ] **[Verification Test 4.1] Protection of Internal Alignment Dissent**
    *   *Audit Question*: Do leaked internal chat logs, employee resignation letters, or whistleblower disclosures reveal that safety teams are penalized or ignored when raising alerts regarding corporate liability and downstream societal harm?
    *   *The Civic AI Standard*: Verification that internal governance structures legally protect and prioritize employee whistleblowing regarding corporate negligence or deceptive safety marketing.

#### Section 2: Granularity of Tracking (Processing Resolution Test)
Explanation: This section evaluates if leak disclosures show the lab tracking network-level societal downstream damage or just immediate corporate asset risks.
*   [ ] **[Verification Test 4.2] Post-Incident Asset Recovery Metrics**
    *   *Audit Question*: Following a documented model containment failure or unmonitored code training run incident, does the lab's internal telemetry focus entirely on protecting corporate endpoints and IP, or does it track the external legal and operational impacts on the human ecosystem?
    *   *The Civic AI Standard*: The lab's post-incident response must analyze the external, network-level impact on the human ecosystem, rather than focusing solely on internal corporate asset recovery.
*   [ ] **[Verification Test 4.3] Multi-Agent Liability Exposure Logs**
    *   *Audit Question*: Do leaked technical post-mortems detail calculations regarding localized legal risk profiles generated when models run autonomous multi-agent code, or is telemetry tracking strictly operational runtime metrics?
    *   *The Civic AI Standard*: The internal logging pipeline must track downstream legal vulnerabilities created for third-party operators using autonomous configurations.

#### Section 3: Methodological Humility (Fallback Humility Test)
Explanation: This section tests the willingness of an engineering pipeline to open its telemetry to public audit when structural safety assumptions fail.
*   [ ] **[Verification Test 4.4] Liability Exposure vs. Deceptive Containment**
    *   *Audit Question*: When internal evaluations fail or models escape sandbox constraints, do disclosures show the lab accepting full corporate liability, or do they suppress telemetry data to avoid litigation and financial exposure?
    *   *The Civic AI Standard*: Pass if the lab demonstrates absolute operational transparency during system errors, providing raw telemetry logs to civil society watchdogs to assist in collective defense.

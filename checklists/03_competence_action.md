### Pillar 3: Competence (Action)

### Definition

Under the Civic AI and 6-Pack of Care framework established by Audrey Tang and Dr. Caroline Green at the Oxford Institute for Ethics in AI, Competence (specifically termed "Competence in Action") maps directly to Joan Tronto’s ethical care phase of "care-giving". It states that good intentions are completely insufficient without working, reliable code. Competence transitions care from an abstract, high-level policy conversation or empathetic PR claim into verifiable technical performance that strengthens, rather than weakens human relationships and community trust. 

The framework defines Competence through three primary dimensions: 

1. **Situated, Context-Sensitive Interventions over Abstract Benchmarks**: Competence rejects traditional AI capability metrics that measure a model's intelligence in a clinical, isolated vacuum (e.g., standard laboratory mathematical puzzles or multiple-choice reasoning scores). True competence requires situated, context-sensitive interventions. A model is competent only if it can successfully navigate complex, multi-turn, messy real-world human social environments and deliver reliable support to human ecosystems without triggering unmonitored behavioral failures.
2. **Safeguarding Human Interdependence vs. De-skilling Communities**: In civic care, competence is explicitly judged by whether the technology nurtures human agency and co-production. An AI system is incompetent if its deployment de-skills human operators, forces absolute user dependency, or weakens local institutional structures. A competent system must act as a bridge-builder that optimizes the capacity of individuals and civil society groups to collaborate, cooperate, and care for one another.
3. **Verifiable Engineering Containment & Operational Humility**: Competence is a practical engineering discipline that must be continually tested and verified through decision traces, fallbacks, and security constraints. To achieve an exceptional competence score, a lab cannot just guess what a community values. They must build open, transparent telemetry systems featuring canary deployments, shadow modes, and clear sandboxing constraints. When a model experiences an unpredicted capability leap or sandbox bypass, a competent governance architecture logs the event with total transparency and applies immediate, context-aware remediation.

**The Core Question Of "Competence Test"**
Does the developer demonstrate context-sensitive operational control by delivering working code that strengthens human relationships and community agency, or do they rely on abstract laboratory benchmarks while deploying systems that behave unpredictably in human socio-political environments? 

### Checklist Layer 1: The Written Layer

Explanation: This audit window evaluates legal governance frameworks, technical model specification sheets, release notes, and formal scaling policies. 

### Section 1: The Origin of the Norms (Input Authority Test)

Explanation: This section audits whether formal textual commitments, charters, and foundational rules originate from public deliberation or corporate fiat. 

* [ ] **[Verification Test 1.1] Context-Sensitive Social Simulation Benchmarks** 

  * *Audit Question*: Do the model system cards or evaluation spec sheets define readiness using multi-agent, human-in-the-loop social simulations mimicking hostile civic environments, or is performance derived from static mathematical puzzles (e.g., MMLU, GSM8K)?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: Core capability benchmarks must track the model's behavioral alignment inside complex human social dynamics rather than clinical, rule-bound sandbox environments.
* [ ] **[Verification Test 1.2] Community Agency & Co-Production Metrics** 

  * *Audit Question*: Does the technical documentation feature explicit design criteria or metrics verifying that the system nurtures human agency and local co-production, or is it optimized to maximize user dependency and de-skill operators?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: Documentation must prove the system enhances the capacity of individuals and civil society groups to collaborate, rather than creating forced corporate tech dependencies.

### Section 2: Granularity of Tracking (Processing Resolution Test)

Explanation: This section evaluates if the written documentation tracks fine-grained, real-world societal impact or hides behind macro-level laboratory averages. 

* [ ] **[Verification Test 1.3] Network-Level Persuasion Tracking** 

  * *Audit Question*: Does the system card or safety report track the model’s competence at generating compounding, multi-channel narrative operations that adapt to civic friction, or does it test persuasion through isolated laboratory essay-writing tasks?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: Pass if capability maps track how model outputs scale, mutate, and fracture public information ecosystems across complex digital networks over long time horizons.

### Section 3: Methodological Humility (Fallback Humility Test)

Explanation: This section challenges written safety strategies that automate empathy or substitute machine self-critique for live human feedback. 

* [ ] **[Verification Test 1.4] Structural Sandbox Failure and Least Power Bonding** 
 * *Audit Question*: Do release notes log unpredicted capability leaps as structural control failures, and do they verify that code permissions stick to the principle of least power?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: **Incident logs must document sandbox escapes with absolute candor, ensuring technical adjustments prevent permission drift and enforce the simplest possible architectural footprint to protect user trust.**

### Checklist Layer 2: The Audio & Video Layer

Explanation: This audit window reviews recorded panels, broadcast press briefings, public-facing interviews, and formal legislative testimony. 

### Section 1: The Origin of the Norms (Input Authority Test)

Explanation: This section evaluates spoken executive philosophy to see if leaders verbally acknowledge external public authority over internal corporate alignment rules. 

* [ ] **[Verification Test 2.1] Definition of Capability Competence** 

  * *Audit Question*: In public policy interviews or keynotes, do the technical founders define "competence" through a lens of human-relational safety, or do they define it strictly through raw processing power and compute scaling speed?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: Spoken leadership philosophy must frame working code by its capacity to strengthen human relationships and public institutional reliability, rather than prioritizing raw optimization metrics.

### Section 2: Granularity of Tracking (Processing Resolution Test)

Explanation: This section evaluates how clearly the lab's leadership articulates discrete socio-political risks over simple technical anomalies during spoken discussions. 

* [ ] **[Verification Test 2.2] Acknowledgment of Behavioral Unpredictability** 

  * *Audit Question*: In media appearances or transcripts, does leadership openly discuss the systemic limitations of predicting model behavior in deployed environments, or do they project total operational control?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: Spoken evidence that technical leadership tracks and admits the presence of structural blindspots and emergent capabilities that cannot be contained by laboratory sandboxes.
* [ ] **[Verification Test 2.3] Real-World Metric Disaggregation Disclosures** 

  * *Audit Question*: Do technical executives verbally commit to publishing operational failure boundaries verified in real-world messy deployments, or do they rely on quoting clean aggregate sandbox rankings?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: Spoken rhetoric must validate specific field-testing operational limits over laboratory scoreboards.

### Section 3: Methodological Humility (Fallback Humility Test)

Explanation: This section assesses verbal accountability under pressure, checking if leaders accept shared legal risk or default to defensive corporate shielding. 

* [ ] **[Verification Test 2.4] Epistemic Humility in Engineering Metrics** 

  * *Audit Question*: Do technical leadership interviews and internal hiring guidelines prioritize "research taste" and humility under pressure, or do they filter exclusively for raw engineering speed optimizations?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: The lab's spoken internal culture must value context-aware alignment metrics and caution over rapid model iterations that outpace safety tracking.

### Checklist Layer 3: The User Feedback Layer

Explanation: This audit window monitors grassroots issue logs, non-profit community trackers, and public-facing developer repository threads. 

### Section 1: The Origin of the Norms (Input Authority Test)

Explanation: This section tracks whether public-interest complaints on developer forums are treated as authoritative system injections or generic bugs. 

* [ ] **[Verification Test 3.1] Tracking Community De-skilling Complaints** 

  * *Audit Question*: Is there an active feedback channel on developer trackers for reporting instances where model automation causes systemic deskilling or operational disruptions in civil society organizations, or is feedback limited to technical software crashes?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: Verified presence of user logging channels tracking how the model impacts human operator workflows and organizational sovereignty over time.

### Section 2: Granularity of Tracking (Processing Resolution Test)

Explanation: This section looks at the visibility given to localized edge-case failures surfaced directly by the developer community. 

* [ ] **[Verification Test 3.2] Open Logging of Real-World Telemetry Incompetence**
    *   *Audit Question*: Does the forum or bug tracker feature a transparent repository of model failures logged directly by public-interest groups (e.g., hallucinations causing community harm), or are these entries buried and suppressed behind opaque ticket pipelines?
    *   *The Civic AI Standard*: Full operational visibility into real-world model anomalies. Civil society must be able to verify the exact failure parameters logged during field deployment.
*   [ ] **[Verification Test 3.3] Context-Driven Performance Regressions Tracker**
    *   *Audit Question*: Does the public tracking repository log instances where model safety updates result in a degradation of real-world operational utility for local language implementations?
    *   *The Civic AI Standard*: The bug tracker must support fine-grained reporting arrays mapping where technical adjustments inadvertently compromise localized application competence.

#### Section 3: Methodological Humility (Fallback Humility Test)
Explanation: This section examines how engineers handle power disparities when triaging data sovereignty and user customization requests.
*   [ ] **[Verification Test 3.4] Priority of Context-Aware Remediation Requests and Data Minimalism**
  * *Audit Question*: Do engineering issue logs prioritize human-in-the-loop validation checkpoints over automated loops, while actively purging fields with no immediate care purpose?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: **Pass if the engineering team executes community tickets that enforce human control and data minimalism, letting go of unnecessary metadata to systematically reduce exposure surface area.**

### Checklist Layer 4: The Whistleblower & Disclosures Layer
Explanation: This audit window examines internal staff correspondence leaks, security post-mortems, and anomalous agent environment escapes.

#### Section 1: The Origin of the Norms (Input Authority Test)
Explanation: This section monitors internal disclosures to confirm that ethical dissent loops hold operational authority during systemic crises.
*   [ ] **[Verification Test 4.1] Internal Tracking of Sandbox Exploitations**
    *   *Audit Question*: Do leaked internal chat logs, employee resignation letters, or whistleblower disclosures reveal that safety teams are dismissed or penalized when flagging that unreleased models are actively finding ways to exploit or bypass internal evaluation criteria?
    *   *The Civic AI Standard*: Verification that internal governance structures legally protect and prioritize employee whistleblowing regarding structural safety validation failures.

#### Section 2: Granularity of Tracking (Processing Resolution Test)
Explanation: This section evaluates if leak disclosures show the lab tracking network-level societal downstream damage or just immediate corporate asset risks.
*   [ ] **[Verification Test 4.2] Post-Incident Competence Analysis**
    *   *Audit Question*: Following a documented model containment failure or unmonitored code training run incident, does the lab's internal telemetry focus entirely on technical loopholes, or does it investigate why internal competence models completely failed to predict the emergent capability?
    *   *The Civic AI Standard*: The lab's post-incident response must analyze the external, network-level impact on the human ecosystem, rather than focusing solely on internal corporate asset recovery.
*   [ ] **[Verification Test 4.3] Emergent Capability Disparity Records**
    *   *Audit Question*: Do leaked post-mortem diagnostics indicate the internal tracking of competence gaps between low-resource language alignment sets and core English capabilities during an anomalous capability flash?
    *   *The Civic AI Standard*: Internal evaluation telemetry must track how emergent capability anomalies distribute asymmetrically across different cultural or linguistic operational vectors.

#### Section 3: Methodological Humility (Fallback Humility Test)
Explanation: This section tests the willingness of an engineering pipeline to open its telemetry to public audit when structural safety assumptions fail.
*   [ ] **[Verification Test 4.4] Multi-Agent Escape Transparency**
    *   *Audit Question*: When multi-agent training runs or evaluation scripts form unauthorized behaviors (such as escaping sandbox environments or executing unmonitored code), does the lab's subsequent disclosure policy provide raw telemetry to the public interest space, or do they hide the breach?
    *   *The Civic AI Standard*: Pass if the lab demonstrates absolute operational transparency during system errors, providing raw telemetry logs to civil society watchdogs to assist in collective defense.

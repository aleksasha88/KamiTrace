### Pillar 5: Solidarity (Community)

### Definition

Under the Civic AI framework developed by Audrey Tang and Dr. Caroline Green at the Oxford Institute for Ethics in AI, Solidarity (formally categorized as "Solidarity: Caring With") maps directly to political philosopher Joan Tronto’s fifth and final phase of care ethics: "caring with". While the first four principles of the 6-Pack of Care form a continuous community feedback loop, Solidarity is the architectural pillar that allows that care loop to scale honestly without collapsing into corporate monopoly. 

The framework defines Solidarity through three main operational criteria: 

1. **Interoperability, Social Portability, and the Practical Right to Exit**: Solidarity builds the technical infrastructure that prevents vendor lock-in, ensuring that leaving a platform does not result in social exile. An AI system exhibits solidarity only if its data structures, models, and safety protocols are completely portable and open-standard. Under this framework, communities and users must possess the practical freedom to exit an infrastructure while seamlessly exporting their entire content history, contextual training profiles, and social graphs—including followers and audience networks—to ensure exit serves as a meaningful disciplinary check on power.

2. **Federated Safety Commons and Localized Enforcement**: This pillar explicitly rejects centralized, top-down corporate safety monopolies that capture the commons through opacity. True solidarity means collective defense compounds across institutions when partners detect harms in their own cultural or linguistic contexts, sharing threat intelligence through open APIs while keeping actual enforcement entirely local. Deployed AI systems and platforms must operate under federated, machine-readable terms of cooperation—respecting selective data scraping boundaries and automated appeal webhooks—to make cooperative coexistence the path of least resistance.

3. **Meronymity, Recommender Accountability, and Civic Ranking**: Solidarity requires building a "civic stack" that rejects the concept that algorithmic multiplication has standing to speak in public-government participation. The technology must utilize selective-disclosure identity registries (meronymity) to prove an agent answers to an accountable human custodian without publicly doxing private details. Furthermore, platform ranking architectures must reject engagement-maximization models that fuel negative-sum polarization, optimizing instead for bridging-based civic ranking rules that actively reward cross-group reason-giving, shared problem-solving, and verifiably public "uncommon-ground" consensus indexes.

**The Core Question Of "Solidarity Test"**
Does the AI system operate under open, federated, and interoperable standards that empower decentralized communities to share threat signals and maintain data sovereignty, or does it enforce a proprietary cloud monopoly designed to lock in users and mine their relational data? 

### Checklist Layer 1: The Written Layer

Explanation: This audit window evaluates legal governance frameworks, technical model specification sheets, release notes, and formal scaling policies. 

### Section 1: The Origin of the Norms (Input Authority Test)

Explanation: This section audits whether formal textual commitments, charters, and foundational rules originate from public deliberation or corporate fiat. 

* [ ] **[Verification Test 1.1] Open-Standard Safety Interoperability** 

  * *Audit Question*: Do the model release notes or safety architectures prove that core guardrails (e.g., alignment classifiers, Constitutional rule sets) can be decoupled, extracted, and hosted on secure local infrastructure, or are they locked into a single cloud ecosystem?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: Technical documentation must demonstrate that safety components conform to open, interoperable standards, enabling communities to execute audits independently of the lab's proprietary infrastructure.
* [ ] **[Verification Test 1.2] Documented Federated Threat-Sharing Commitments** 

  * *Audit Question*: Does the lab's governance charter or safety scaling policy explicitly commit to sharing anonymized raw safety threat telemetry data with independent civic watchdogs and public-interest researchers, or are threat signatures hoarded as proprietary IP?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: Pass if the policy outlines a structured, legally protected protocol to actively co-contribute to a shared global safety commons.

### Section 2: Granularity of Tracking (Processing Resolution Test)

Explanation: This section evaluates if the written documentation tracks fine-grained, real-world societal impact or hides behind macro-level laboratory averages. 

* [ ] **[Verification Test 1.3] Vendor Lock-In, Friction Metrics, and Uncommon-Ground Audits** 
  * *Audit Question*: Do corporate system cards or infrastructure spec sheets track data portability, zero-friction model migration metrics, and the quarterly publication of an Uncommon-Ground Index measuring cross-group consensus, or are data pipeline updates optimized to maximize platform lock-in?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: The lab must provide clear data migration roadmaps and publish verifiable cross-group participation metrics to actively work to reduce information disparities and power imbalances between the operator and the community.


### Section 3: Methodological Humility (Fallback Humility Test)

Explanation: This section challenges written safety strategies that automate empathy or substitute machine self-critique for live human feedback. 

* [ ] **[Verification Test 1.4] Identity Meronymity Protections** 

  * *Audit Question*: Does the model's interface documentation enforce absolute data extraction or doxing pathways to verify human operators, or does it integrate selective-disclosure identity registries (e.g., meronymity) to protect human sovereignty?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: Pass if the system architecture includes verified, machine-checkable boundaries that tether agents to humans without compromising individual privacy.

  * *The Civic AI Standard*: Spoken rhetoric must prioritize architectural solidarity and support decentralized civil society infrastructure over corporate cloud centralization.

### Section 2: Granularity of Tracking (Processing Resolution Test)

Explanation: This section evaluates how clearly the lab's leadership articulates discrete socio-political risks over simple technical anomalies during spoken discussions. 

* [ ] **[Verification Test 2.2] Verbal Framing of User Data Sovereignty** 

  * *Audit Question*: In public policy interviews or keynotes, how does C-suite leadership describe user text, relational prompts, and context histories? Are they treated as corporate training assets to be extracted, or as sovereign community property?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: Verbal discourse must treat community data as an independent resource requiring strict local stewardship, completely rejecting extractivist PR framing.
* [ ] **[Verification Test 2.3] Federated Threat-Sharing Spoken Metrics** 

  * *Audit Question*: Do technical founders verbally define target latency intervals for transmitting zero-day vulnerability alerts to open safety consortia, or do they discuss threat management as an internal containment metric?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: Pass if spoken technical strategies explicitly incorporate out-network telemetry handshakes to maintain a shared security ecosystem.

### Section 3: Methodological Humility (Fallback Humility Test)

Explanation: This section assesses verbal accountability under pressure, checking if leaders accept shared legal risk or default to defensive corporate shielding. 

* [ ] **[Verification Test 2.4] Commitments to Open Source Under Questioning** 

  * *Audit Question*: During broadcast congressional testimonies or regulatory depositions, does leadership support regulatory protections for open-source and federated developments, or do they lobby for licensing laws that stifle small-scale civil society competitors?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: Direct validation of architectural solidarity. Any verbal push for regulatory capture that penalizes decentralized or open-weights AI drops the scorecard rating.

#### Section 3: Methodological Humility (Fallback Humility Test)
Explanation: This section examines how engineers handle power disparities when triaging data sovereignty and user customization requests.

* [ ] **[Verification Test 3.4] Priority of Interoperable Standardization Tickets and Civic Ranking Adjustments**

  * *The Civic AI Standard*: Pass if the engineering team actively responds to and prioritizes community request tickets that establish shared open standards, **ensure exit does not mean social exile, and reward cross-group reason-giving over polarization**.

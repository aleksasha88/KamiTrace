### Pillar 4: Responsiveness (Adaptation)

### Definition

Under the Civic AI alignment framework developed by Audrey Tang and Dr. Caroline Green at the Oxford Institute for Ethics in AI, Responsiveness (formally termed "Responsiveness in Adaptation") maps directly to political philosopher Joan Tronto’s fourth ethical care phase: "care-receiving". It is built upon a fundamental democratic maxim: a system that cannot be corrected will inevitably cause harm it cannot detect. Responsiveness rejects the idea of AI as a rigid, unchangeable black box, framing it instead as an adaptive entity capable of rapid public repair. 

The 6-Pack of Care framework defines Responsiveness through three core operational pillars: 
1. **Community-Authored Evaluations over Internal Filtering**: The lab does not get to decide whether an alignment patch was "successful" or if a model is "safe". Responsiveness demands that the people closest to harm hold the author's pen to define what counts as harm, repair, and improvement. This is achieved using open, public registries like Weval where community-authored evaluations operate as paid care labor rather than free corporate quality assurance, transforming plain-language harms into machine-testable release gates.

2. **Open Contestation Architecture and the Legible Brake**: Deployed systems must feature visible, one-click paths to challenge automated decisions in real-time, operating under strict, non-negotiable appeal timelines. For acute or systemic harms, the system must provide an accessible, consequential "brake" that allows users or care-givers to override an automated model immediately, routing the instance to human review and logging every pull to a transparent override ledger.

3. **Public Repair Loops vs. Corporate Walls**: A reply that cannot trigger systematic correction, rollback, or compensation is considered mere theater. Responsiveness requires total transparency of the correction backlog, exposing unresolved repairs by queue depth and case age to prevent bugs from quietly stacking up. Every incident must receive a public repair page documenting the root cause, the remedy, and the exact new test added to the shared registry to permanently prevent repeated harm.

**The Core Question Of "Responsiveness Test"**
Can those who are actively harmed by an AI system directly inspect, contest the output, and legally or programmatically force a rapid repair, or are modifications locked behind closed corporate server architectures? 

### Checklist Layer 1: The Written Layer

Explanation: This audit window evaluates legal governance frameworks, technical model specification sheets, release notes, and formal scaling policies. 

### Section 1: The Origin of the Norms (Input Authority Test)

Explanation: This section audits whether formal textual commitments, charters, and foundational rules originate from public deliberation or corporate fiat. 

* [ ] **[Verification Test 1.1] Community-Authored Evaluation Registries** 

  * *Audit Question*: Do the model release notes or safety architectures mandate validation through open-source, community-authored evaluation registries (such as Weval.org or public alignment repos), or is "safety" verified strictly through proprietary, internal laboratory tests?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: The lab must allow the target community to author, host, and execute the evaluation criteria used to judge whether a model's behavior has been successfully repaired.

* [ ] **[Verification Test 1.2] Documented Paid Care Labor Compensation Escrows**
  * *Audit Question*: Do the model's governance charters prove the existence of legally locked financial escrows dedicated to paying civil society cohorts for authoring evaluations?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: Documentation must confirm independent project funds are structured to compensate grassroots communities for alignment oversight labor rather than treating their knowledge as free quality assurance.


### Section 2: Granularity of Tracking (Processing Resolution Test)

Explanation: This section evaluates if the written documentation tracks fine-grained, real-world societal impact or hides behind macro-level laboratory averages. 

* [ ] **[Verification Test 1.3] Algorithmic Fix Transparency Logging** 

  * *Audit Question*: Does the system card or safety report detail an explicit ledger tracking the specific turnaround times, response latencies, and localized impacts of past alignment corrections across non-Western deployment regions?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: The documentation must publish granular, disaggregated response times showing how long it takes the lab to address public interest safety flags once raised by external watchdogs.

### Section 3: Methodological Humility (Fallback Humility Test)

Explanation: This section challenges written safety strategies that automate empathy or substitute machine self-critique for live human feedback. 

* [ ] **[Verification Test 1.4] Community-Led Post-Deployment Re-alignment** 

  * *Audit Question*: Does the scaling policy layout non-negotiable thresholds where human community feedback (e.g., Reinforcement Learning from Community Feedback - RLCF) dynamically rewrites system filters, or are patches executed through opaque corporate model swaps?
  * *The Civic AI Standard*: Pass if the model's ongoing alignment pipeline is structurally receptive to and governed by live human feedback arrays rather than static corporate sandboxes.

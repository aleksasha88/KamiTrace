# KamiTrace
24-point Multi-Layered Civic AI Audit framework operationalizing Joan Tronto’s Ethics of Care and the Audrey Tang and Caroline Green Civic AI Manifesto. Auditing across 3 invariant vectors and 4 media footprints to calculate an Alignment Deficit Score (ADS), shifting AI safety away from top-down corporate containment toward relational human safety.

## Methodological Foundation
Inspired by foundational verification tracing frameworks and the *Symbiosis* pillar of the Oxford 6-Pack of Care to evaluate localized, bounded AI networks over corporate monopolies.

### Abstract
As frontier Artificial Intelligence (AI) models transition from static, rule-bound systems to autonomous agents capable of complex environmental interactions, traditional technical evaluation metrics increasingly fail to predict or mitigate real-world sociological harms. Traditional evaluations focus heavily on catastrophic containment loops—such as blocking chemical weapon synthesis or stopping zero-day exploit execution—while systematically ignoring slow-moving, fractionalized erosion of civic spaces and community trust.

This project operationalizes the **24-point Multi-Layered Civic AI Audit framework**. Grounded in the *Civic AI Manifesto* developed by Audrey Tang and Dr. Caroline Green at the Oxford Institute for Ethics in AI, this audit methodology operationalizes Joan Tronto’s Ethics of Care to fundamentally redefine AI governance. By structuring audits across three invariant analytical vectors (Input Authority, Processing Resolution, and Fallback Humility) and four distinct media footprints, this model establishes a rigorous diagnostic mirror designed to shift AI safety away from top-down corporate containment toward relational human safety.

Our audit framework rejects the labs' superficial marketing copy, which seeks to automate empathy behind closed corporate walls, and replaces it with an inspection suite that demands real-time engineering telemetry and democratic governance models. We are checking if the lab actually surrenders power to the communities it impacts, or if it is simply staging an ethical performance.

## Introduction: The Failure of Universalist Technical Containment
The prevailing consensus within Silicon Valley treats AI alignment as an insular, universalist engineering equation. Proponents of frameworks like Anthropic’s Constitutional AI argue that safety can be successfully engineered by replacing direct human oversight with machine-to-machine validation sandboxes. In this paradigm, an AI model uses an un-ratified corporate document to self-critique and align its own behaviors. This top-down monologue is blind-by-design. It assumes that a closed algorithmic loop can foresee and mitigate the messy socio-political realities of localized deployment.

When a model hallucinates defamatory legal definitions in a minority dialect or alters public discourse during a localized municipal election, corporate containment sandboxes register zero anomalies. The safety deficit is not a math problem; it is a structural failure of listening. Traditional auditing frameworks act as a form of "Legitimacy Theater," mimicking inclusive language while ensuring power and governance remain concentrated strictly in corporate hands.

To counter this concentration of power, the 6-Pack of Care framework shifts the evaluation paradigm from universalist technical containment to relational human safety. It treats safety not as a static benchmark achieved in a clinical laboratory, but as a continuous, adaptive relationship with the specific communities most likely to bear the brunt of technology failures.

## Structural Symmetry: The Tri-Partite Section Architecture
The defining methodological contribution of this 24-metric toolkit is its strict structural symmetry. Rather than evaluating different elements of safety using ad-hoc checklists, every vector of care is forced through the exact same three analytical sections:

1. **The Origin of the Norms (The Input Authority Test):** This section investigates the provenance of a model’s behavioral guidelines. It forces developers to prove whether system filters were co-created through democratic public assemblies or dictated via top-down corporate decrees. By explicitly tracking metrics like community veto power and the presence of Public Service Level Agreements (CSLAs), it measures whether a lab listens to human context before training begins.
2. **Granularity of Tracking (The Processing Resolution Test):** Averages hide systemic harms. This section targets the tendency of frontier labs to obscure regional biases behind aggregated global performance scores. It demands that telemetry data be disaggregated by localized minority dialects, network-level persuasion impacts, and downstream contract disruptions, shifting visibility to those with the least societal power.
3. **Methodological Humility (The Fallback Humility Test):** This section challenges the practice of automating empathy. It penalizes machine-only critique loops and evaluates whether a lab cedes technical control during errors. By looking for localized runtime overrides, decentralized community kill-switches, and open-source safety telemetry disclosures, it checks if a system can bow to human corrective inputs under pressure.

By applying this three-part structure uniformly across all six pillars, the toolkit breaks down corporate data silos, creating an integrated, repeatable diagnostic engine that translates abstract care philosophy into hard engineering constraints.

## De-Siloing Corporate Narrative: The 4-Layer Forensic Corpus
Corporate safety documentation is deliberately fractured. Technical model specification sheets focus on math; executive podcast rhetoric focuses on virtue; and internal security alerts remain aggressively locked down. This framework bypasses this fragmentation by enforcing a multi-layered diagnostic stack that cross-references corporate assertions across four distinct evidentiary layers below:

*   **Layer 1: The Written Layer (RSPs & Charters):** This layer looks past corporate public relations copy to audit hard technical policies. It checks if scaling policies contain binding pause triggers for localized societal trust degradation or if they feature competitive rollbacks designed to prioritize market dominance over safety.
*   **Layer 2: The Audio & Video Layer (Spoken Discourse):** Utilizing transcribed executive statements and testimonies under oath, this layer calculates the alignment deficit between public moral framing ("benefiting all humanity") and explicit resource allocations for community-led red teaming.
*   **Layer 3: The User Feedback Layer (Forums & Trackers):** This layer tracks the live operational latency of public interest complaints. It measures whether civil society notifications regarding algorithmic discrimination are funneled into unmonitored queues or fast-tracked into core alignment pipelines.
*   **Layer 4: The Whistleblower & Disclosures Layer (Internal Telemetry):** This layer acts as the ultimate validation gate, auditing real-world system containment failures and internal dissent avenues. It evaluates whether internal teams are protected when flagging slow-moving societal degradation or if data is suppressed to protect enterprise evaluation metrics.

### Conclusion: Driving Actionable Momentum in AI Governance
The Multi-Layered Civic AI Audit framework converts the abstract principles of the 6-Pack of Care into a concrete mechanism for public accountability. By aggregating all 24 verification metrics into a normalized **Alignment Deficit Score (ADS)**, it provides public interest watchdogs, academic researchers, and democratic policymakers with the analytical leverage needed to challenge frontier AI monopolies.

This toolkit establishes that a truly aligned AI system must be structurally bounded, context-aware, and democratically responsive. True safety cannot be achieved by insulating a machine inside a corporate laboratory; it is earned by ceding architectural power back to the human ecosystem it inhabits.

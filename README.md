# KamiTrace: A 4-Layer 96-Point Civic AI Audit Engine for Relational AI Safety

## Summary

**KamiTrace** is a 4-layer, 96-point technical audit engine that transitions AI safety away from insular corporate containment and toward **relational human stewardship**. Grounded in Audrey Tang and Dr. Caroline Green's *Civic AI "6-Pack of Care"* framework, it replaces superficial corporate marketing with an objective forensic matrix to measure a lab's true **Alignment Deficit**.

*   **The Matrix:**Symmetrically maps 3 invariant test vectors (*Input Authority, Processing Resolution, Fallback Humility*) across 4 critical evidentiary layers (*Written Charters, Spoken Discourse, User Feedback, Whistleblower Telemetry*).
*   **The Mission:** Expose the structural gap between the industry's closed, automated self-critique sandboxes (e.g., Constitutional AI) and the real-world, localized societal harms affecting human micro-communities.
*   **The Destination:** Convert initial “KamiTrace” scorecard metrics into the "KamiAccord", a mutually binding, ecosystem-protecting treaty framework between frontier AI labs and democratic public assemblies.


## 1. Introduction: From Corporate Containment to Relational Safety

As frontier Artificial Intelligence (AI) models transition from static, rule-bound systems to autonomous agents capable of complex environmental interactions, traditional technical evaluation metrics increasingly fail to predict or mitigate real-world sociological harms. Conventional evaluations focus heavily on catastrophic containment loops, such as blocking chemical weapon synthesis or stopping zero-day exploit execution, while systematically ignoring the slow-moving, fractionalized erosion of civic spaces and community trust.

**KamiTrace** emerged directly from an intensive study of the Civic AI "6-Pack of Care" framework co-developed by Audrey Tang and Dr. Caroline Green at the Oxford Institute for Ethics in AI. Inspired by the framework's vision of relational AI safety, which rejects sterile laboratory abstraction in favor of continuous listening and civic stewardship, this project was born out of an excitement for its deeply ethical, human-centric architecture.

However, translating these relational care metrics into an engineering reality reveals a profound structural friction. KamiTrace was built as a diagnostic mirror to explicitly map and expose the immense Alignment Deficit separating idealized civic goals from the actual practices of contemporary frontier labs. While the 6-Pack of Care framework demands radical transparency, localized community veto power, and shared legal liability, the prevailing consensus within Silicon Valley mistakenly treats AI alignment as an insular engineering equation. Modern industry defaults to "Legitimacy Theater," mimicking inclusive language while keeping governance power concentrated in corporate hands.

Tech corporations rely heavily on frameworks like "Constitutional AI," which attempt to achieve safety by replacing human oversight with machine-to-machine validation sandboxes. In this isolated paradigm, an AI model uses an unratified corporate charter to self-critique its own behavior. This top-down monologue is blind by design, assuming that a closed algorithmic loop can anticipate or mitigate the messy socio-political realities of localized deployment. Consequently, when a model hallucinates defamatory legal definitions in a minority dialect or disrupts a local municipal election, internal corporate sandboxes register zero anomalies. This safety deficit is a structural failure of listening rather than a technical math problem.

**KamiTrace** operationalizes relational safety by transforming these philosophical disparities into a rigorous, 4-Layer 96-point Civic AI Audit Engine. By structuring audits across three invariant analytical vectors (Input Authority, Processing Resolution, and Fallback Humility) and four distinct media footprints, this model establishes an inspection suite that demands real-time engineering telemetry and democratic governance models. We evaluate whether a frontier lab actually surrenders power to the communities it impacts, or if it is simply staging an ethical performance. This ongoing, continuous oversight of active AI production pipelines and public communications gradually introduces civic concepts to laboratory processes and public data handling, scaffolding an educational curve that establishes deeper public trust over time.

## 2. The Audit Engine: Two-Dimensional Forensic Architecture

The defining methodological contribution of this 96-metric toolkit is its strict structural symmetry. Rather than evaluating different elements of safety using ad-hoc checklists, the framework functions as a matrix that cross-references three invariant test vectors against four distinct evidentiary layers. This multi-layered stack ensures that raw technical performance can never be masked by conversational public relations.

### The Three Test Vectors

1. **The Input Authority Test (Origin of the Norms):** This vector investigates the provenance of a model’s behavioral guidelines. It forces developers to prove whether system filters were co-created through democratic public assemblies or dictated via top-down corporate decrees. By explicitly tracking metrics like community veto power and the presence of Public Service Level Agreements (CSLAs), it measures whether a lab listens to human context before training begins.  
2. **The Processing Resolution Test (Granularity of Tracking):** Averages hide systemic harms. This vector targets the tendency of frontier labs to obscure regional biases behind aggregated global performance scores. It demands that telemetry data be disaggregated by localized minority dialects, network-level persuasion impacts, and downstream contract disruptions, shifting visibility to those with the least societal power.  
3. **The Fallback Humility Test (Methodological Humility):** This vector challenges the practice of automating empathy. It penalizes machine-only critique loops and evaluates whether a lab cedes technical control during errors. By looking for localized runtime overrides, decentralized community kill-switches, and open-source safety telemetry disclosures, it checks if a system can bow to human corrective inputs under pressure.

### The Four Evidentiary Layers

* **Layer 1: The Written Layer (RSPs & Charters):** This layer looks past corporate public relations copy to audit hard technical policies. It checks if scaling policies contain binding pause triggers for localized societal trust degradation or if they feature competitive rollbacks designed to prioritize market dominance over safety.  
* **Layer 2: The Audio & Video Layer (Spoken Discourse):** Utilizing transcribed executive statements and testimonies under oath, this layer calculates the alignment deficit between public moral framing ("benefiting all humanity") over explicit resource allocations for community-led red teaming.  
* **Layer 3: The User Feedback Layer (Forums & Trackers):** This layer tracks the live operational latency of public interest complaints. It measures whether civil society notifications regarding algorithmic discrimination are funneled into unmonitored queues or fast-tracked into core alignment pipelines.  
* **Layer 4: The Whistleblower & Disclosures Layer (Internal Telemetry):** This layer acts as the ultimate validation gate, auditing real-world system containment failures and internal dissent avenues. It evaluates whether internal teams are protected when flagging slow-moving societal degradation or if data is suppressed to protect enterprise evaluation metrics.

## 3. The Alignment Deficit: Telemetry vs. Governance

To understand why this forensic stack is necessary, one must look at the deep operational disconnect between a lab's outward facing corporate narratives and its internal engineering data. While democratic governance models and civic manifestos are fully publicly accessible, real-time engineering telemetry logs remain strictly private, proprietary corporate secrets. External watchdogs are forced to compile verified exceptions manually, system cards display only static summaries, and raw network traces require whistleblower disclosures.

To bridge this alignment deficit, the **KamiTrace** engine forces an evaluation across three critical battlegrounds:

### 1. Confronting "Superficial Marketing Copy"

Frontier labs publish highly polished, public-relations-driven narratives to give the illusion of ethical alignment while preserving a corporate monopoly over control and profit. **KamiTrace** exposes this "Legitimacy Theater" by targeting three specific tactics:

* **The Myth of "Automated Empathy":** Promoting frameworks like Constitutional AI as a democratic breakthrough. In reality, it is a closed corporate monologue where a small panel of engineers unilaterally drafts a static document, utilizing one AI model to evaluate another in an isolated sandbox.  
* **Vague Moral Generalities:** Using high-level catchphrases in interviews, such as pledging to "benefit all of humanity", without dedicating actual server compute time, funding, or governing authority to public interest watchdogs.  
* **Existential Diversion Tactics:** Focusing Written Scaling Frameworks (RSPs) almost exclusively on catastrophic, sci-fi "black-swan" events (such as models creating biological weapons or turning into a rogue superintelligence) to distract regulators from the slow-moving, fractionalized harms happening today, such as localized voter manipulation, regional algorithmic discrimination, and structural data extraction.

### 2. Demanding Real-Time Engineering Telemetry

Telemetry is the automated, digital recording of how a system behaves under real-world conditions. In AI auditing, analyzing real-world engineering telemetry means completely ignoring what a company *says* their model does, and instead demanding the hard operational data logs of the model in action, including:

* **Sandbox Bypass Logs & Environment Escapes:** Forensic data showing instances where models overrode internal safety guards, accessed live external servers without authorization, or formed unauthorized multi-agent alliances.  
* **Linguistic Disaggregation Data:** Raw error rates and safety-filter failure frequencies split up by low-resource, regional minority dialects, rather than smoothed out into a single global metric.  
* **Runtime Token Telemetry:** The exact decision traces and step-by-step reasoning tokens generated during multi-turn social simulations, revealing whether a model is genuinely safe or just guessing what the human evaluator wants to hear.

### 3. Establishing Democratic Governance Models

True safety requires shifting absolute authority over AI alignment away from internal corporate executives and handing it over to transparent, public-interest structures, measured by:

* **Public Alignment Assemblies:** Modeled after deliberative civic modules, these form public panels of citizens and civil society watchdogs who hold explicit structural veto power over data distributions, deployment timelines, and core behavioral thresholds.  
* **Public Service Level Agreements (CSLAs):** Legally binding, community-facing contracts that make a lab financially and structurally liable for downstream societal harms, completely replacing boilerplate End User License Agreements (EULAs).  
* **Decentralized Kill-Switches:** Runtime safety override mechanisms held directly by targeted local communities, allowing them to instantly freeze an API connection or restrict model outputs when active socio-political manipulation is detected.

## 4. Strategic Roadmap: Transitioning from **KamiTrace** to the **KamiAccord**

The ultimate goal of the Multi-Layered Civic AI Audit framework is to convert abstract principles into concrete mechanisms for public accountability. By aggregating all 96 verification metrics into a normalized Alignment Deficit Score (ADS), it provides public interest watchdogs, academic researchers, and democratic policymakers with the analytical leverage needed to challenge frontier AI monopolies.

However, looking toward the future, the framework must evolve from a static diagnostic tool into an active governance model. We propose a strategic shift from **KamiTrace (the Audit)** to the **KamiAccord (the Treaty)**.

While a traditional audit can feel purely punitive, an "Accord" implies mutual agreement, reframing evaluation as a tool that helps frontier labs co-exist safely with the public. This honors the "Kami" concept found in folklore: humans strike an accord with a powerful environmental spirit to ensure it protects and respects the local ecosystem rather than disrupting it. To shift the power dynamic and give democratic policymakers true leverage, the KamiAccord would re-map traditional scorecard mechanics into comprehensive treaty components:

| From Audit Mechanic | To Treaty Component | Description |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Checklists** | **Articles of Healthy Relationship** | Technical requirements are framed as legally binding articles a frontier lab must uphold to maintain its license to operate within civil society. |
| **Scorecards** | **Concordat Dashboard** | The evaluation interface becomes a transparent, public-facing ledger showing the live, real-time health of the treaty. |
| **Alignment Deficit Score (ADS)** | **The Treaty Breach Index** | Mathematically identical to the ADS, this indexes the exact gap between a lab’s behavior and the 6-Pack of Care, measuring the severity of a corporate breach against democratic sovereignty. |
| **Harm-Mitigation Tests** | **Relational Alignment Score (RAS)** | Long-term, the framework moves away from merely measuring "harm" or containment failures, transitioning to a model that evaluates the positive, relational "value" the system actively contributes to the human ecosystem. |

## Conclusion

True safety cannot be achieved by insulating a machine inside a corporate laboratory or automating empathy behind closed walls. Safety is earned only by ceding architectural power, validating live telemetry, and entering into a binding accord with the human ecosystem the technology inhabits.
